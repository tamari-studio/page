# Tamari: the story

One line: **Tamari is where a team's work pools: its tools, its notes and its data in one calm place
that it owns.**

## The arc

**1. The word.** Tamari (溜まり) comes from the Japanese verb *tamaru*: to gather, to collect, to
pool, the way rainwater settles into a clear pond. The word has two lives. One is a place: a
*tamariba* (溜まり場) is the spot where people end up together without being told to, and in sumo
the *tamari* is the ringside area where wrestlers wait and gather focus before their bout. The other
is a substance: tamari is the dark, concentrated liquid that pools at the bottom of a barrel of
miso as it ages.

**2. Where our name comes from.** This product was built under the name *miso*. Tamari is what
miso gives you when you leave it to do its work: the part that collects by itself, slowly, and is
richer than anything that went in. We didn't pick a Japanese word for its look. We picked the word
for what our own project turned out to make.

**3. The problem.** A growing team's work scatters. Every need gets its own small app: one for
the pipeline, one for the plan, one for the notes, one for the numbers. Each has its own login,
its own copy of the data and its own idea of the truth. What the team knows ends up spread across
a dozen tabs, and some of it sits on someone else's servers.

**4. What Tamari does.** Tamari gives the team one place for that work. A tool earns its place
there as a space: its pages, its dashboard cards and the data it needs, built by the team or by the
team's AI, and used by everyone at the role they have. Notes, shared records and history collect in
the same place, and the team's AI reads the same pool through one connection. Every company gets
its own copy and its own database, so the pool stays theirs.

**5. The promise.** Small things add up. Each tool, note and record a team adds makes the next one
easier to find and more useful, the way tamari concentrates over time. The place stays quiet while
that happens: no feed to keep up with, no extra app to open.

## The story for an About page or a pitch deck

> In Japanese, *tamari* (溜まり) comes from *tamaru*: to gather, to pool. It names a place where
> people naturally come together, and the rich liquid that collects at the bottom of a barrel of
> miso as it ages.
>
> We started this product under the name miso. What it turned into is the tamari: the place where a
> team's work pools.
>
> Growing teams scatter their work across small apps, each with its own login and its own copy of
> the truth. Tamari brings it back together. Each tool the team needs becomes a space, built by the
> team or its AI, next to the team's notes and shared data, on a database the team owns. Small
> contributions collect in one calm place and add up to something worth more than the parts.

## Short forms

- **Hero (current, keep it):** Where your team's software lives.
- **One-liner:** One calm place where your team's tools, notes and data pool together.
- **Name line:** Tamari (溜まり): where things gather and pool.

## How the story shows up in the product

- **Voice:** calm and plain. Sentence case, short sentences, no hype. This already matches the
  product's design rules (`docs/space-design.md` in miso-miso).
- **Look:** still water, not a neon dashboard. Generous space, warm neutrals, ink-dark text, and the
  self-hosted Zen Kaku Gothic New and M PLUS 1 fonts the landing page already uses.
- **Empty states:** quiet and useful, and they say the next step. For example: "Nothing has pooled
  here yet. Add a record, or ask your AI to start one."
- **Pooling as a feature, not just a metaphor:** one search over everything the team has, and the
  team's AI reading the same pool. This is the `search_records` work on branch
  `feat/search-records`.

## Facts the story rests on

- *Tamari* (溜まり) comes from *tamaru* (溜まる), "to collect, to pool". Safe to state.
- Tamari soy sauce began as the liquid that pools during miso making. Safe to state. Say "a barrel
  of miso", not a specific wood or region, unless we check it.
- *Tamariba* (溜まり場) means a hangout, a place where people gather. In everyday Japanese it is
  neutral to slightly informal and can mean the place a group loiters. Use "where people gather"
  and don't call it a lounge or a meeting room.
- In sumo, *tamari* is the ringside waiting area, and *tamari-seki* are the ringside seats. Keep it
  as background colour, not the headline.

## What we left out, and why

- **The "docs, whiteboards and tasks" positioning** from the research. Tamari has no whiteboard, and
  copying an all-in-one suite is Macro's fight, not ours. Our hook is that the team's own tools live
  here, built by them and their AI, on data they own.
- **Three separate brand angles** (gathering space, compounding knowledge, calm readiness). They
  are now one arc: the place (beat 1) holds the pooling (beats 4 and 5), and calm is the tone of the
  whole thing.
- **Japanese styling as decoration.** Use the kanji once, where we explain the word. No brush
  strokes, no faux-Japanese typefaces, no other borrowed motifs.

## Open points

- The name isn't cleared yet. A naming round found no identical EU or US marks in software classes,
  but a lawyer should check TAMARIS (Wortmann KG) and TAMARIN (Chameleon Games). The exact-match
  .com, .app, .io and .dev domains are taken.
- The app, CLI and repos still say miso. For now only the landing page says Tamari.
